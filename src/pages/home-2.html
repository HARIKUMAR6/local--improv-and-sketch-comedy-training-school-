Step into improv

Comedy is a skill anyone can learn

We teach improv and sketch comedy as a craft you practise, not a talent you either have or don’t. Every class is built on three verbs: listen, play, and write.

What the work builds

Listening
Every scene starts with what your partner just said. You learn to hear it, accept it, and build on it.
Collaboration
Improv is a team sport. You practise making your scene partners look good instead of protecting your own idea.
Spontaneity
Games and exercises train you to make quick, specific choices and commit to them before you overthink.
Character
You develop characters with a clear point of view and learn to play them truthfully in scenes and sketches.
Writing
In sketch, you turn a funny idea into a script, then rewrite it until it works in front of people.
Confidence
Taking small risks in a supportive room makes it easier to speak up, try an idea, and keep going when it doesn’t land.

From first class to spotlight

From your first warm-up game to a live audience

Training follows the same arc at every level: learn the fundamentals, practise them until they feel natural, then put them in front of people.

  1. 01 · Discover

    Learn the fundamentals

    Start with warm-up games and simple scenes. You don’t need any experience: there are classes for complete beginners.

    • Warm-up and listening games
    • Saying “yes, and”
    • Simple two-person scenes

  2. 02 · Practise

    Build your skills

    Work on scenes, characters, and sketch writing with working comedians who give you notes as you go.

    • Scene work and characters
    • Writing and rewriting sketches
    • Notes from working comedians

  3. 03 · Perform

    Take it to the stage

    Stage time is built into every term, so you perform for a live audience while you are still training.

    • Improvised sets
    • Sketch performances
    • A real, live audience
